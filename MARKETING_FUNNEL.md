## Email 2 — Useful Insight (sends ~3 days later)

**Subject:** The pattern nobody clocks in themselves

Hi {{first_name}},

Of the seven in the guide, the one people are worst at spotting in
themselves is **the Competence–Confidence Split**.

The short version: competence is built by doing the work. Confidence is a
*belief* about yourself, and beliefs don't update just because evidence
piles up — only because you actually let that evidence count. A lot of
capable people quietly installed a rule early on: *this one doesn't count
yet.* It's a great engine for effort. It's a terrible way to ever feel done
proving yourself.

If that lands, here's the question the guide asks about it: **what would
have to be true for your last result to actually count?**

Sit with that one for a minute before you answer it. Most people notice the
honest answer is "nothing would" — which is the pattern talking, not the
truth.

— Hazik

## Email 3 — Story (sends ~7 days later)

**Subject:** What ten years of watching people under pressure taught me

Hi {{first_name}},

Before any of this, my job was cabin crew — and for a good part of it, a
Line Check Cabin Crew Evaluator, which meant watching crew perform during
real flights and assessing what I saw. Not a classroom. Live operations,
real stakes.

After enough of that, you stop seeing individual people and start seeing
patterns. The same handful, over and over: the capable one who freezes, the
competent one who over-explains, the person who was right and said nothing.
None of it correlated with skill. What was actually running underneath was
something quieter — a conclusion someone drew once, in a moment that
mattered, and never revisited.

That's the whole premise behind the guide, and behind how I coach: the
pattern isn't a character flaw. It's just old and unexamined. [I wrote more
about where this noticing habit actually started](https://thealtitudemindset.com/blog-path-through-distraction.html)
if you want the longer version.

— Hazik

## Email 4 — The Framework (sends ~11 days later)

**Subject:** Noticing the pattern isn't the same as changing it

Hi {{first_name}},

The guide is built to help you *see* a pattern. Seeing it is necessary, but
it's not the same as changing it — that takes something more structured,
which is why every coaching engagement I run follows the same five stages:

**Recognize** → name the specific belief, trigger or behaviour underneath
the situation you thought was the problem.
**Reframe** → most limiting beliefs are conclusions you drew once, never
facts. Take them apart and look at what else is true.
**Reset** → learn to shift out of a reactive state deliberately, on demand.
**Rewire** → build the new response using practical NLP, so it holds up in
the moment, not just in theory.
**Rise** → apply it consistently until it's just how you operate.

[The full framework is here](https://thealtitudemindset.com/framework/) if
you want to see how each stage actually works in a session.

— Hazik

## Notes for whoever implements this

- Timing (3 / 7 / 11 / 14 days) is a reasonable starting cadence, not a
  fixed rule — most providers let you A/B the delay.
- Every link above points to a real, live page — verified against the
  current sitemap.
- Nothing in this sequence promises a result, a number, or a guarantee.
  Keep it that way if you edit these — it's consistent with the rest of the
  site's voice and with the "Confidence Guarantee" wording already in use
  elsewhere (which is about refunds, not outcomes).
- Do not add a client testimonial to any of these emails until a real one
  exists. See `AUDIT.md` §9/§14 — the site has deliberately shipped without
  fabricated social proof so far; this sequence should hold that same line.
