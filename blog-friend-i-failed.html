The Friend I Failed: A Letter I'll Never Send

By Hazik Fayaz

I recall a great friend I lost to life a decade ago.

I claimed I was a brother. A best friend. Everything.

But was I really a good friend?

When he was with me—yes. I might have been the best person to him. And he was the only friend and family to me. My go-to person. Everything I could recall.

But as I went through life, I realized something that still haunts me:

After he left, I wasn't there for him.

I can't explain it in words, but he had left a trail of events for me—small tests, quiet moments, chances to prove my friendship.

And I lost them all.

Maybe because I got too busy in life proving my worth to the world.

Maybe because I thought our friendship was solid enough to survive my absence.

Maybe because I didn't realize that love—even the deepest kind—needs tending.

As I reflect now, that pain of failing as a friend has more weight than anything else in Life.

More than the money I lost.

More than the plans that fell apart.

More than the voices I couldn't reclaim.

This one sits differently.

Because I wasn't failed by someone else. I failed someone who trusted me.

I am guilty of not being there.

I just hope he forgives me.

And the Family I hurt—the one that had expectations of me, and I couldn't be There.

I am Sorry, My Friend.

I don't know if you'll ever read this.

I don't know if it would change anything if you did.

But I need to say it, even if just to the void:

You deserved better.

You deserved the friend I claimed to be.

You deserved someone who showed up, not just when it was easy, but when it mattered.

And I wasn't that person.

This is the Essence of Life, I think.

We fail people. Not always out of malice. Sometimes out of distraction. Sometimes out of survival. Sometimes because we're so busy trying to prove ourselves to the world that we forget to prove ourselves to the people who already believed in us.

But that doesn't erase the failure.

It just makes it heavier.

If I could go back, I would choose differently.

I would see the trail you left.

I would recognize the tests for what they were—not obstacles, but invitations.

I would show up.

But I can't go back.

All I can do is carry this forward:

The knowledge that friendship isn't just what you feel. It's what you do.

And I didn't do enough.

To anyone reading this who's carrying the weight of a friendship they failed:

You're not alone in this guilt.

Maybe you got too busy. Maybe you didn't see the signs. Maybe you thought there would be more time.

Maybe, like me, you were the one who was left behind—but you were also the one who didn't reach back.

It's a hard truth to hold.

I don't know if forgiveness comes for failures like this.

I don't know if time heals it, or if it just teaches you to live with the weight.

But I know this:

If I ever get another chance—with another friend, another family, another person who believes in me—

I won't lose the trail.

I won't let the world drown out what matters.

I won't fail them the way I failed you.

My Friend,

Wherever you are, whatever you've built without me—

I hope you're okay.

I hope you found people who showed up.

I hope you forgive me, even if I don't deserve it.

And I hope you know:

I see it now. What I lost. What I failed to be.

And I'm sorry.

I'm so sorry.
